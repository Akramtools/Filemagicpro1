Free Excel to PDF Converter with Automatic Print-Fit

Anyone who has printed a wide Excel report knows the frustration: the sheet looks fine on screen, but the PDF or printout splits columns across two or three pages, cutting data in half. Excel's desktop "Fit to 1 page wide" print setting solves this, but most online Excel-to-PDF converters skip it entirely, exporting a fixed font size regardless of how many columns your sheet has. This tool replicates that fit-to-page behavior in the browser: it measures your sheet's column widths against the selected page size, then automatically calculates the largest font size that lets every column sit within one page's width — so you get a clean, single-width PDF instead of columns sliced across pages.

How Print-Fit Scaling Works

After you upload a file, the tool reads every column's content width at the page's available printable area (page width minus margins). It tests font sizes from your chosen base size downward, checking at each step whether the full row of columns fits within that width. As soon as it finds the largest size that fits, it locks that size in for the whole table, keeping every column readable at a consistent scale rather than shrinking some columns more than others. If your sheet has so many columns that even a small, readable font size can't fit them all on one page, the tool won't shrink further into unreadable text — switching to "Actual size" mode instead lets the table span multiple pages at full size, which is often more usable for extremely wide datasets.

Choosing the Right Settings for Your Sheet

For most invoices, reports, and data exports with 5–15 columns, "Fit all columns to page width" with Landscape orientation on A4 gives the most readable single-page result. For narrower sheets with just a few columns, Portrait orientation wastes less paper. If you're archiving a very wide sheet (30+ columns) for reference rather than printing, "Actual size" mode preserves full readability at the cost of extra pages. The header-row toggle is worth keeping on for any sheet longer than one page, since it repeats your column titles at the top of every page rather than leaving later pages unlabeled.
